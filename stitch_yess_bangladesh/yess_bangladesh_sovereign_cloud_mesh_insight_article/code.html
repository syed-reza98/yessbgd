Sovereign Cloud Mesh Architecture: Resilient Multi-Region Infrastructure for Bangladesh

The Sovereign Imperative in National Digital Services

Over the past decade, Bangladesh's digital economy has expanded at an extraordinary pace, powering everything from micro-merchant financial switchboards to nationwide agricultural logistics. However, this critical layer of national stability remains disproportionately anchored to offshore hyperscaler infrastructure located across regional transit hubs in Singapore, Mumbai, and Frankfurt.

This operational architecture introduces acute vulnerabilities: exposure to submarine cable disruptions, sudden foreign exchange billing fluctuations, and regulatory non-compliance with Bangladesh Bank's statutory data residency mandates. Egress fees alone bleed millions in hard foreign currency reserves annually, while network latency spikes above 65ms during cross-border transit choke high-frequency fintech clearing pipelines.

To establish technological self-determination, institutional enterprises and state institutions require an architectural blueprint engineered from the ground up: the Sovereign Cloud Mesh (SCM). By unifying domestic tier-3 data centers into an active-active distributed fabric, Bangladesh can retain complete sovereign authority over citizen telemetries while maintaining microsecond responsiveness.

Decoupled Topology: Bare-Metal Kubernetes & Domestic Edge Nodes

The foundation of the Sovereign Cloud Mesh departs from proprietary hypervisors, relying instead on high-throughput, bare-metal Kubernetes clusters interconnected across domestic National Internet Exchanges (NIX). The physical topology pairs the primary Dhaka node at Mohakhali BNDC Tier-3 with secondary high-availability nodes situated at the Jashore Software Technology Park and Sylhet High-Tech City.

Through this decoupled paradigm, data processing occurs within immediate geographic proximity to the transaction origin. This directly mitigates packet traversal over international waters and completely eradicates unpredictable FX-denominated cloud egress invoices.

Zero-Trust Service Mesh & Distributed Cryptography

Security in a sovereign perimeter cannot presume internal network integrity. The framework enforces strict Zero-Trust Architecture through an automated Mutual TLS (mTLS) service mesh, utilizing SPIFFE/SPIRE for dynamic workload identity attestation. Every microservice invocation is cryptographically authenticated with ephemeral keys rotated every 15 minutes.

Kernel-level observability is maintained via enhanced Berkeley Packet Filter (eBPF) telemetry agents. This provides granular deep-packet inspection without introducing memory-copy bottlenecks, guaranteeing compliance with national cybersecurity audits in real-time.
